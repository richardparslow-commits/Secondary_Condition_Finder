## Evidence-led promotion

### `femoroacetabular-impingement` → `osteoarthritis`

A systematic review of 35 studies found that certain cam-type femoroacetabular-impingement features, particularly elevated alpha angle, predispose selected patients to radiographic progression of hip osteoarthritis. The evidence for pincer-type impingement was less well established. The mapping therefore requires same-hip morphology, imaging chronology, and review of alternative hip-OA causes and does not treat all FAI phenotypes as causal.

Clinical source: Kowalczuk et al., *Sports Med Arthrosc Rev.* 2015; PMID 26524551.

## Safe-draft dispositions retained

The other 13 Wave 2 identities remain unpublished after re-screen:

- `charcot-arthropathy`: ulceration, infection, deformity, and amputation are important clinical consequences, but the current catalog does not provide a sufficiently specific downstream disease target for a general publication without phenotype/site overreach.
- `sacroiliac-joint-dysfunction`: no distinct downstream catalog disease met the condition-specific publication threshold.
- `hip-labral-tear`: treatment reviews discuss osteoarthritic progression, but the available evidence does not cleanly establish labral-tear-primary natural-history causation independent of FAI, dysplasia, and other structural hip abnormalities.
- `greater-trochanteric-pain-syndrome`: systematic reviews characterize pain, abductor dysfunction, gait changes, and treatment outcomes rather than a sufficiently specific downstream disease relationship.
- `patellar-instability`: evidence linking management strategies to later patellofemoral OA is treatment-confounded and methodologically limited; it is not sufficient for a general instability-primary OA mapping.
- `tarsal-metatarsal-malunion-nonunion`: current meta-analysis supports post-traumatic OA after medial tarsometatarsal/Lisfranc injury and non-anatomic reduction, but the catalog identity is established malunion/nonunion; those phenotypes are not interchangeable.
- `radial-neuropathy`, `median-neuropathy`, `tibial-neuropathy`, and `femoral-neuropathy`: CRPS type II can follow a known nerve injury, but these catalog neuropathies are etiologically broader than traumatic nerve injury and cannot safely be converted into generic CRPS pathways.
- `meralgia-paresthetica`: systematic reviews focus on lateral-femoral-cutaneous-nerve anatomy and treatment response rather than a distinct downstream disease target.
- `complex-regional-pain-syndrome`: current systematic-review evidence does not support converting depression/anxiety into routine CRPS-primary secondary mappings, and CRPS manifestations themselves are not separate downstream catalog diseases.
- `cranial-nerve-palsy`: the identity is too heterogeneous across nerve, cause, and phenotype for a general downstream disease relationship without a more specific target model.
