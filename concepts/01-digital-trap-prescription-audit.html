Concept 1 — The Digital Trap & The Prescription Audit

Close-up, shallow depth of field, hand-hewn dark mesquite conference table. 1600×1000 (16:10). Everything above is a rendered frame, not a photograph — use it as an art-direction proof, a layout reference for a photo shoot, or a comp for review.

Read order

1 · The trapCrimson banner is the first thing the eye finds — the algorithm has already decided. Screen is the brightest object, so it wins the tonal hierarchy.
2 · The auditThe leather ledger cuts across the tablet's lower edge, physically interrupting the machine. Lone Star and stitching read as hand-tooled, not printed.
3 · The groundFlag, limestone, dust and amber flare hold the top third at low contrast so the foreground separation survives at thumbnail size.

Shot notes

Subject distanceMacro-close. Tablet occupies roughly 45% of frame width; notebook is the nearest object and is cropped by the bottom edge; flag and wall hold focus falloff.
Optics85mm macro at f/1.8–f/2.8 (or 50mm at f/1.4) on full frame, sensor plane parallel to the tablet so the screen text stays legible while the wall dissolves.
LightSingle hard late-afternoon window at ~3200K raking from camera-right at 15–20cm above the table plane, one silver card left as fill so the leather keeps texture. Screen contributes its own cool ~6500K rim on the notebook edge.
ExposureProtect the crimson: expose for the alert banner and let the limestone sit a stop hot. Keep the screen at 85–90% so it reads as emissive without clipping to paper white.
Text safetyThe alert wording is a regulated-sounding claim about a real underwriting decision. Shoot the screen blank or with abstract UI, then composite the exact copy in post so the wording is version-controlled — see the prompt pack for the no-text variant.
Series frames1a tighter macro on the banner only; 1b notebook-forward with the tablet demoted to background bokeh; 1c the flag dominant with the table as a warm horizon; 1d overhead flat-lay for print and web hero crops.
